Commercial Real Estate Bridge Financing: A Complete Guide

January 15, 2026 • Education

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loans have revolutionized rental property financing by allowing investors to qualify based on property cash flow rather than personal income. Here's everything you need to know.

What is a DSCR Loan?

A DSCR loan is a type of investment property mortgage that qualifies borrowers based on the rental income generated by the property, not the borrower's personal income or tax returns. The lender calculates the property's debt service coverage ratio to determine if the rental income can cover the mortgage payment.

How DSCR is Calculated

The formula is simple:

DSCR = Monthly Rental Income ÷ Monthly Debt Service (PITI)

For example, if a property generates $2,500/month in rent and the mortgage payment (including taxes and insurance) is $2,000/month:

DSCR = $2,500 ÷ $2,000 = 1.25

Minimum DSCR Requirements

Most lenders require a minimum DSCR of 1.0 to 1.25. A DSCR of 1.0 means the rental income exactly covers the mortgage payment. Higher ratios indicate stronger cash flow and typically qualify for better rates.

Key Benefits

No Tax Returns Required: Perfect for self-employed investors or those with complex tax situations.

Unlimited Properties: Unlike conventional loans, there's typically no limit on how many DSCR loans you can have.

Cash-Out Refinancing Available: Access your equity without income verification.

Who Should Consider a DSCR Loan?

DSCR loans are ideal for experienced real estate investors who have strong rental properties but don't want to verify personal income. They're particularly valuable for investors with multiple properties, self-employed individuals, or those looking to scale their portfolios quickly.

How to Finance Your First Office Building Acquisition

January 10, 2026 • Case Study

Meet Sarah, a real estate investor who transformed her small portfolio of 3 rental properties into a thriving 15-property business in just 18 months. Here's how she did it using strategic financing.

The Starting Point

Sarah owned 3 single-family rentals in her local market, all with conventional 30-year mortgages. While the properties cash flowed well, she hit a wall: conventional lenders told her she'd maxed out at 10 financed properties total, and her debt-to-income ratio was too high to qualify for more loans.

The Strategy Shift

Sarah discovered DSCR loans and realized she could qualify based on rental income alone. She developed a clear acquisition plan:

Properties 4-6: Used DSCR loans to purchase three turnkey rentals in growing Sun Belt markets. Each property had a DSCR above 1.25, making financing straightforward.

Properties 7-10: Leveraged a portfolio loan to refinance her original 3 properties plus purchase 1 new one. This consolidated her financing and freed up capital.

Properties 11-15: Combined DSCR loans with a revolving line of credit for down payments, allowing her to move quickly on opportunities.

The Numbers

Total portfolio value: $3.2M

Monthly cash flow: $8,500

Average equity per property: $85,000

Time to scale: 18 months

Key Lessons

1. Don't let conventional lending limits stop you. Alternative financing products exist for exactly this scenario.

2. Cash flow is king. Every property Sarah purchased had strong fundamentals and positive cash flow from day one.

3. Speed matters. Having pre-approval and ready access to capital meant Sarah could close deals quickly in competitive markets.

Capital Markets Update: Commercial Real Estate Lending Trends

January 5, 2026 • Market Analysis

As we enter 2026, real estate investors face a dynamic market with both opportunities and challenges. Here's our analysis of key trends and what they mean for your investment strategy.

Interest Rate Environment

After the volatility of recent years, interest rates have stabilized in the 6-7% range for investment properties. While higher than the historic lows of 2020-2021, rates are predictable and sustainable for cash-flowing deals.

Our take: Don't wait for rates to drop. Focus on properties that work at today's rates—if rates do decline, you can refinance and boost your returns.

Supply and Demand Dynamics

Housing inventory remains tight in most markets, keeping competition high for quality properties. However, we're seeing increased opportunities in:

Secondary Markets: Cities like Boise, Nashville, and Raleigh continue to show strong fundamentals with better price points than coastal markets.

Multifamily: The rental demand remains robust, particularly for well-located 5-20 unit properties.

Value-Add Opportunities: Properties needing light renovation are seeing less competition than turnkey homes.

Financing Trends

DSCR and portfolio loans continue to gain popularity as investors seek alternatives to conventional financing. Lenders are becoming more competitive, leading to better terms and faster closings.

Our 2026 Predictions

1. Rental demand stays strong as housing affordability challenges keep homeownership out of reach for many.

2. Cash flow becomes even more critical as speculation cools and investors focus on fundamentals.

3. Creative financing grows as savvy investors use tools like DSCR loans and lines of credit to move quickly.

Bottom Line

2026 favors prepared, well-financed investors who can act quickly on quality deals. The key is focusing on cash-flowing properties in strong rental markets and having your financing lined up before you find the perfect property.
